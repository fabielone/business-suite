# Route work to teams, keep one owner

**Yes, add Teams. Build them as a routing and visibility layer, not as a new kind of owner.** A team says *where work waits*. A person says *who is doing it*. Almost every service desk, IT tool and shop-dispatch product works this way: one field for the group or queue, and one optional field for the person. "Unassigned" simply means the team is set and the person is empty ([Zendesk](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4409149119514-About-omnichannel-routing-with-unified-agent-status), [ServiceTitan](https://help.servicetitan.com/docs/dispatch-and-track-your-technicians)). The earlier chat answer was mostly right: `teamId` plus a nullable `assigneeId`, server-checked claims, offline claims kept as "pending", roles kept apart from teams, a team queue, a Team target in automations, and a My work view. This report corrects five details. First, the claim must **not** use the current workspace-wide revision check, because any unrelated edit would make a claim fail. Second, "lead" must become a per-team relation. Third, the command name `task.release` clashes with the existing `order.release`, so call it `task.unclaim` ("give back"). Fourth, team management belongs in Settings, while My work belongs in the phone bottom bar. Fifth, the product is not "a routing layer over business records". It is **job/shop management software** with a light back office, and the Teams feature is one part of it. Before any of this, fix a real security hole in today's code: `task.save` only checks `chores.write`, so a production worker can edit or reassign *any* task by its id, including tasks they cannot see.

## Every serious tool splits "which team" from "which person"

The evidence is consistent across products. Zendesk says a ticket "must be assigned to a group to be eligible for routing", and the system then picks a person inside that group ([Zendesk](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4409149119514-About-omnichannel-routing-with-unified-agent-status)). ServiceNow incidents carry an *assignment group* plus an *assigned to* person, and the product counts each reassignment ([ServiceNow docs](https://www.servicenow.com/docs/r/platform-administration/c_MetricDefinitionSupport.html)). Field-service tools work the same way. In ServiceTitan, removing a technician sends the job back to an "Unassigned" tray ([ServiceTitan](https://help.servicetitan.com/docs/dispatch-and-track-your-technicians)). Jobber shows a red "Unassigned" icon on visits nobody holds ([Jobber](https://help.getjobber.com/es/articles/visits/)). Linear gives each team a Triage inbox, and one key press, `I`, means "assign yourself" ([Linear](https://linear.app/docs/triage), [Linear assigning](https://linear.app/docs/assigning-issues)).

There are three other ways to build this, and each one has a known weakness. **Option A: put the team in the assignee field**, as ClickUp and monday.com do. It causes friction: in monday.com, an automation that set a team replaced the person already there ([monday.com community](https://community.monday.com/t/assigned-person-gets-replaced-when-adding-a-team-by-automation/65829)), and ClickUp users keep asking for effort and due dates per person ([ClickUp feedback](https://feedback.clickup.com/feature-requests/p/ability-to-split-effort-among-team-members)). **Option B: owner is a person *or* a queue, never both**, as in Salesforce. When someone accepts the item, the team link is lost ([UMN guide](https://it.umn.edu/services-technologies/how-tos/enterprise-crm-service-console-manage-0)). That is worse for a shop, where a claimed Production job should still count as Production's work. **Option C: many assignees on one task**, as in Planner, Basecamp and Odoo. Here "done" is shared: in Planner, "when any team member marks the task complete, it's marked complete for all" ([Microsoft](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/planner/assign-people-to-tasks)). The model the evidence supports is the Zendesk/ServiceNow one: **team stays set, person is optional, and claiming fills in the person.**

The reason to move work quickly from "the team's" to "one person's" is human, not technical. In a field experiment, requests sent to one person got more replies, and better ones, than the same request sent to a group. The authors explain this as *diffusion of responsibility*: everyone assumes someone else will answer ([Barron & Yechiam 2002](https://datalearner.com:8443/academic/journal-papers/0747-5632/volumes-and-issues/72/paper-detail/71520)). Asana allows only one assignee per task for the same reason, "so there's no confusion about who's responsible" ([Asana](https://asana.com/resources/why-one-assignee)). So an unclaimed team item is a **waiting state, not an ownership state**. It needs a visible age and a named person who answers for it, normally the team lead.

### "Someone should do it" and "everyone must do it" are different records

There are two kinds of team work, and they need different shapes. A **shared task** ("someone in Production should cut the panels") is one row: `teamId` set, `assigneeId` empty until someone claims it, and done when the claimer finishes. A **fan-out task** ("everyone in Production must read the new safety rule") is a parent row plus one child row per team member. Each child already has its person set, so nobody needs to claim anything and no claim race can happen. The parent shows progress such as "4 of 6 done". This is Asana's own answer: "assign copies" to a whole team creates one task per person ([Asana copies](https://asana.com/de/inside-asana/assign-copies-of-a-task.md)). Planner users who need per-person completion fall back to duplicates or checklists ([Microsoft Q&A](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/4425497/planner-tasks-for-multiple-person)). Make the children from the team list **at the moment of creation** (a "snapshot"). People who join later do not get a child added automatically, and people who leave keep theirs, flagged for the lead. No product surveyed documents a better rule, and the snapshot never changes records silently.

## The repo has one assignee, no teams, and an open write hole

Today, `Task` and `Job` each have exactly one `assigneeId`, which points to a Contact with `kind: 'member'` or is empty. There is no team, claim, `completedBy` or `completedAt` field, and no `teams` collection in `SuiteData` (`packages/core/src/operations/types.ts:72-80, 145-154, 264-283`). "Up for grabs" can already be written as an empty `assigneeId`. In practice it does not work, because the read filter shows production workers only work assigned to them, so nobody can browse a pool (`packages/core/src/operations/production.ts:119-138`).

**The security gap comes first.** Every `task.*` command requires only `chores.write` (`permissions.ts:57-86`), and `production` has `chores.write` (`permissions.ts:26-56`). `authorizeCommand` adds a record check only for `job.move`, `task.complete` and `notification.read`. "Every other command checks only the action permission and has no record scope" (`production.ts:87-89`). So a production worker can send `task.save` with any task id, change the assignee to themselves, and so take or rewrite tasks they are not allowed to see. That breaks the project rule "enforce … record scope on the backend. UI hiding is not security" (AGENTS.md). A second, smaller bug: `task.save` accepts the Money sentinel `'pool'` (shared funds) as an assignee, while `job.save` rejects it (`commands.ts:781, 1049`). Teams must not reuse `'pool'`.

There are four more mismatches. **Lead scope:** the docs say a team lead sees "work assigned to their team" (`docs/requirements/business-suite permissions.md:72`), but in code the `lead` role has full `work.read` and can complete any task. The source comment even says "Team leads see all production work" (`permissions.ts:53`). Teams make "their team" checkable for the first time. **Completer:** decision BS-DEC-032 is resolved with the rule "Record who completed each task" (`open decisions.md:44`), yet `task.complete` only sets `done = true` (`commands.ts:1075-1101`), and the core audit type has no actor (`types.ts:177-186`). **Jobs live inside Orders:** a Job sits in `Project.jobs[]` and has no `version` of its own (`types.ts:90-103`), so claiming a Job means changing the Order row. **One revision for the whole workspace:** `expectedRevision` is compared to a single workspace number, and a mismatch returns "This workspace changed in another tab…" (`commands.ts:282-287`). There is good news here too. The API locks the workspace row (`SELECT … FOR UPDATE`) for each command (`apps/api/src/operations.ts:313`). That means commands run one at a time, and a "still unclaimed?" check inside the command already gives a clean **first claim wins** result.

Navigation is a flat list: Overview, People, Companies, Pipeline, Jobs, Money, Tasks, Referrals, Automations, Device sync, Activity, Settings. On phones, the first four entries go in the bottom bar and the rest under "More" (`apps/web/src/operations/OperationsApp.tsx:69-82, 982-983`). There is no My work or Teams page, and the Tasks table has no "mine" or "unassigned" filter (`pages.tsx:1280-1307`). One more thing to keep in mind: there are two "member" concepts, accounts (`suite_space_members`, one role each) and member Contacts (the assignment targets), and they are linked only by matching email (`production.ts:35-48`).

## The recommended design: teams route, roles permit, one person owns

### Data

Add one record type and a few fields. Keep them specific, because the project rules forbid "universal entity tables" and generic workflow engines (AGENTS.md; `docs/requirements/business-suite architecture.md:88`).

| Thing | Fields to add | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Team** (new `teams` collection) | `Row` + `name`, `memberIds[]` (member Contact ids), `leadIds[]`, `archived` | Team members are member Contacts, the same ids every assignee field already uses |
| **Task** | `teamId` (optional), `claimedAt`, `completedBy`, `completedAt`, `parentTaskId` (for fan-out children) | Team set + person empty = up for grabs; `completedBy` fixes the BS-DEC-032 gap |
| **Job** (inside Order) | `teamId`, `claimedAt` | Same pattern for production work; written through the Order row |
| **Assignment history** (append-only) | `at, recordId, fromTeam, toTeam, fromAssignee, toAssignee, actorId, reason` | ServiceNow's experience shows that rebuilding "time to claim" and "bounce count" from a general audit table is fragile ([ServiceNow community](https://www.servicenow.com/community/platform-analytics-articles/measuring-incident-reassignments-to-the-service-desk-or-any/ta-p/2302954)) |
| **Automation action** | a separate `teamId` target, not a `team:<id>` string | Avoids another magic string like `'pool'` |

A person can be on **several teams**, and can be lead of one team and a normal member of another. Lead is stored *on the team*, not in the person's role. This is the "relationship" style used by OpenFGA and Zanzibar, where "member of team T" is a fact that permission checks look up ([OpenFGA](https://openfga.dev/docs/modeling/user-groups)). It also avoids what OWASP calls "role explosion", for example separate "sales-lead" and "production-lead" roles ([OWASP](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Authorization_Cheat_Sheet.html)). One naming warning: an **"Admin" team** (office work) is easy to confuse with admin *permissions*. Call it "Office" or "Admin office", and make clear in the UI that a team gives no permissions.

### Commands and who may run them

Split assignment out of `task.save`, so that each action gets its own server check. These rules go into `authorizeCommand` and `projectForActor` in shared core, so example mode and the API both apply them automatically (`production.ts:53-161`).

| Command | Allowed when (checked on the server, against *current* membership) |
|---|---|
| `task.save` | Edits title, notes and due only. A non-manager may edit only tasks they can see. It no longer changes `assigneeId` or `teamId` for production/lead users |
| `task.claim` | Actor has `chores.write`, is a member of the task's team, **and the task is still unclaimed**. Sets `assigneeId = me`, `claimedAt` |
| `task.unclaim` ("give back") | Actor is the assignee, or a lead of that team, or a manager. Returns the task to the team queue, with an optional reason |
| `task.assign` | Manager (`work.assign`), or a lead of the task's team. The target must be a member of that team, or the team is changed at the same time |
| `task.complete` | Assignee, lead of the task's team, or manager (CRM-T-07). Records `completedBy` and `completedAt` |
| `team.save` / `team.archive` | `members.manage` |

The claim's check is **"is it still unclaimed, and am I on this team?"**, not "has anything in the workspace changed?". With today's single workspace revision, a claim sent with `expectedRevision` would fail every time anyone edited anything. The check belongs on the record's state. If tasks later move to their own database rows, the same rule becomes one conditional update, `UPDATE … WHERE id=$id AND assignee_id IS NULL`. Under PostgreSQL's READ COMMITTED setting, the losing request then changes zero rows ([PostgreSQL](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/transaction-iso.html)). `SKIP LOCKED` is the wrong tool for a person claiming one specific item. PostgreSQL itself says it gives "an inconsistent view" and suits only "queue-like" consumers that just want the next free row ([PostgreSQL](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/sql-select.html)). It would fit only a future "take next" button. Return a clear, typed answer to the loser, such as "already claimed by Marta", rather than a generic error.

### What people can see

Today, workers see only their own items. Widen this to **own items + unclaimed items in my teams**. Team leads see **everything in their team**. Managers and owners see everything. Following Zendesk users' advice, keep "Mine" and "Unassigned in my teams" as **separate lists**, because one mixed list is awkward to work from ([Zendesk community](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/community/posts/4409515287450-Assign-ticket-to-a-group-of-agents/comments/4409509901594)). Whether normal members can also see teammates' *claimed* items is an open owner decision. The safe default is no, and it can be opened later. **Team membership never unlocks prices**: price fields stay tied to `prices.read` (`production.ts:139-150`), so a production person on the Sales team still sees no deal amounts. The same visibility rule must also decide **what is copied to each device**. Otherwise the local database holds records the API would refuse to show, and as Ink & Switch note, anyone holding a local copy "cannot be prevented from locally modifying it" ([Ink & Switch](https://www.inkandswitch.com/essay/local-first/)).

When someone **leaves a team**, do not silently unassign their claimed work. Flag it for the lead. Planner shows the opposite risk: removing someone from a plan does not remove them from its tasks ([Microsoft](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/planner/assign-people-to-tasks)). For **repeating tasks**, the current code copies `assigneeId` to the next occurrence (`commands.ts:1075-1101`). For a *team* task that someone claimed, the next one should go back to the team queue unclaimed. For a task a manager assigned directly to a person, keep the person.

## Offline claims stay "pending" until the server says yes

The earlier answer was right that an offline claim is a request, not a fact. Popular sync engines all let the server decide. In Rocicorp Zero, a failed server check rolls the change back, and "the optimistic mutation on the client will be reverted" ([Zero](https://zero.rocicorp.dev/docs/mutators)). ElectricSQL sends writes through your API and lists "marking writes as rejected for manual resolution" as one way to handle refusals ([Electric](https://electric.ax/docs/guides/writes)). Plain **last-writer-wins** (the newest save simply overwrites) is fine for notes and is reportedly good enough for Linear ([Fujimon](https://hn.jakechampion.name/reader/https://www.fujimon.com/blog/linear-sync-engine)). For claims it is wrong, because the *last* offline claimer would quietly steal the task. It would also break the project's own rule that a conflict must not be auto-resolved by replacing the expected version (`docs/offline-contract.md:78`).

So the app should show **"Claim pending"** in its own style, not "Mine", until the server confirms. If the server says no, keep a lasting, synced **outcome message**, for example: "Your claim of 'Cut panels for Order 112' at 10:42 was not applied: Marta claimed it at 10:31." Do not simply let server data overwrite the screen, which is PowerSync's default ([PowerSync](https://docs.powersync.com/handling-writes/handling-update-conflicts)). Any work done offline after a refused claim, such as notes, photos or time, must be kept and marked for the real claimer or the lead. Never drop it. That follows the rule "never silently discard unsynced work" (AGENTS.md). Business refusals should come back as normal responses with a typed reason, so the upload queue does not get stuck. PowerSync warns that 4xx answers block its queue ([PowerSync](https://docs.powersync.com/handling-writes/handling-write-validation-errors)). Each queued command already carries an id that stops it being applied twice (`commands.ts:268-281`). Keep that for claim, give-back and complete. Finally, `task.save` is already in the offline command set (`OperationsApp.tsx:57-68`). Once assignment moves out of it, add `task.claim` and `task.unclaim` as their own offline commands, each with its pending state.

## Open queues invite cherry-picking, so design the queue policy

Letting people pick their own work has a measured cost. In a study of 2.4 million radiology diagnoses, doctors who left the set order to pick quicker or similar cases were *less* productive, and picking the shortest tasks first was "particularly detrimental" ([UNC CBOH](https://cboh.unc.edu/publication/discretionary-task-ordering-queue-management-in-radiological-services/)). Support vendors describe the same pattern, called *cherry-picking*: easy tickets get taken, and hard ones wait for days or weeks ([Gladly](https://www.gladly.ai/blog/cherry-picking-tickets-customer-service/)). That is vendor opinion, not independent research. In a small shop, the bigger risk is not lost speed. It is that **the hard or unpleasant job nobody wants sits untouched**.

The fixes are simple and well tried. **Sort the queue by due date, then by age**, never by "easy", as Zendesk, Freshdesk and Front all do by default ([Zendesk](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4409149119514-About-omnichannel-routing-with-unified-agent-status), [Front](https://help.front.com/en/articles/1315904)). **Show an age badge** on each item ("waiting 3 days"). Offer a **"Take next" button** that gives you the top item, like Zendesk's Play mode ([Zendesk Play](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/9186492658714-Using-Play-mode-to-quickly-work-through-tickets)), alongside the free list. Hiding the list completely, as in Zendesk's Guided mode, is an Enterprise-plan control and too heavy for teams of two to five people ([Zendesk Guided](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4408825479066-Setting-up-Guided-mode)). **Escalate unclaimed work**: after a set time, notify the team lead, the same approach as Freshdesk's group rules ([Freshdesk](https://support.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/196581-automatic-ticket-assignment-in-a-round-robin-manner)). For work that is claimed but stuck, warn on *no activity* or a near due date, not on raw hours, because a real fabrication job can sit claimed for days. **Never auto-release a person's claim** in the style of a machine queue timeout ([AWS SQS](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AWSSimpleQueueService/latest/SQSDeveloperGuide/sqs-visibility-timeout.html)). If it is ever added, make it a visible, logged event. A gentle soft limit at claim time ("you already hold 6 open tasks") follows Kanban's idea of pulling work "only if there is capacity" ([Kanban Guide](https://kanban.university/kanban-guide/)). Zendesk, too, lets people go over limits on purpose ([Zendesk](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4409149119514-About-omnichannel-routing-with-unified-agent-status)).

**Auto-assign can come later, and should stay small.** The usual options are *round robin* (take turns in order) and *load balancing* (give it to whoever has the fewest open items). Freshdesk calls round robin "suited to small teams" ([Freshdesk](https://support.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/196581-automatic-ticket-assignment-in-a-round-robin-manner)). Odoo and HubSpot both skip people who are away ([Odoo](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/18.0/applications/services/helpdesk.html), [HubSpot](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/workflows/assign-tickets-using-workflows)). Skill-based engines are enterprise features and too much for four small teams. A shop will most likely mix two styles: the Production lead *pushes* some jobs to people, as on a dispatch board, and staff *pull* the rest from the team tray. The useful numbers to watch are unclaimed age, time to first claim, bounce count and "unclaimed at end of day". ServiceTitan treats zero unassigned jobs at end of day as full coverage ([ServiceTitan](https://help.servicetitan.com/docs/dispatch-and-track-your-technicians)). All of these come from the assignment history log.

### Screens: My work up front, team setup in Settings

**My work** should be the first or second nav entry, so it lands in the phone bottom bar (the bar shows only the first four entries). It has three sections: *Mine*, *Up for grabs in my teams* (with Claim and Take next) and, for leads only, *My team's work*. Zendesk users asked to have "unassigned in your groups" back on their home screen because they had to leave home to find new work ([Zendesk community](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/community/posts/6745361237146-Add-List-of-Unassigned-Tickets-in-Your-Groups-to-Agent-Home/comments/8882118109082)). Creating teams, adding members and choosing leads is a rare admin job. Put it in **Settings next to members and roles**, gated by `members.manage`. Add a team filter to Tasks and Jobs, and show a team chip on each card. A separate **Teams** page that shows each team's queue is optional and can live under "More" on phones. The Jobs page already shows "Unassigned" (`pages.tsx:598, 645`), so it can grow a team tray like Jobber's unscheduled sidebar ([Jobber](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/schedule-overview-new-schedule/)). Check every new screen on narrow and wide screens, as the project rules require.

Write down five decisions before Phase 1, because no document answers them. Can members see teammates' claimed work? Do team memberships attach to member Contacts (recommended, to match assignees) or to accounts? What should the "Admin" team be called? Who is the escalation owner when a team has no lead? The docs name the audiences "manager, sales, marketing and production" (`CRM workflow and terminology.md:12`) but never define departments, queues or claiming, and the role templates are still an open decision (BS-DEC-013).

## What kind of software this is

It is not just a CRM, and "ERP" claims too much. The centre of the product is the loop **lead → order → production job with stages → cost versus agreed price → payment**. That loop is what comparable products sell as *shop management*: shopVOX for sign, print and apparel shops tracks jobs "from design through installation" ([shopVOX](https://shopvox.com/?p=1261)), and JobBOSS² is "job-shop" software for make-to-order work ([ECI](https://ecisolutions.com/products/jobboss2/)). Real ERP grew out of manufacturing planning (MRP) and centres on bills of materials, inventory and accounting ([Gartner Peer Community](https://www.gartner.com/peer-community/discussions/enterprise-resource-planning)), which this app does not. "Business operating system" and "Work OS" are vendor slogans, not real categories ([monday.com](https://monday.com/p/press-kit/)), and "Operations Hub" is already a HubSpot product name for CRM data sync ([HubSpot](https://hubspot.com/products/Operations)). The honest plain label is **"job management software for small shops"**. The longer version: vertical (industry-specific) shop software with a light back office (expenses, approvals, reimbursements) and built-in team work management. The team queues borrow the helpdesk "group queue" pattern and the factory "work centre" idea, where work waits at a station, not with a person ([Odoo work centres](https://odoo.com/documentation/16.0/applications/inventory_and_mrp/manufacturing/management/using_work_centers.html)). Working offline on web, phone and desktop sets it apart from that whole category.

## Conclusion

The main lesson is that "up for grabs" is mostly a *policy* problem, not a data-model problem. Two fields and one server check make claiming work. Whether hard jobs get done depends on sort order, age badges, a named escalation owner and a quick move from "the team's" to "one person's". The second lesson is that adding Teams is also the right moment to make record scope real. Today's code trusts any `chores.write` holder with any task, and it gives every lead the whole shop. Team membership turns "their team" from a word in the docs into a rule the server can check, but only if the write hole is closed first.

Splitting the product's parts (teams route, roles permit, one person owns, money stays behind `prices.read`) also protects the suite against its biggest known risk: departments feeling like separate apps and half the staff avoiding the system ([O2B Technologies](https://www.o2btechnologies.com/blog/odoo/why-your-odoo-implementation-is-not-working)). Odoo keeps its helpdesk teams, sales teams and work centres separate per app ([Odoo](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/saas-18.4/applications/services/helpdesk.html)). One simple, workspace-wide Team record that both Tasks and Jobs point to is a cleaner choice for a shop this size. It lets the owner see in one place what is waiting, where, and for how long.
